Define how organizations should manage AI initiatives as a governed strategic portfolio.

---
# Core Principle

Without portfolio discipline:
AI initiatives become fragmented experiments with unclear value.

---
# Portfolio Lifecycle

Intake
→ Triage
→ Prioritization
→ Evaluation
→ Deployment
→ Monitoring
→ Retirement

---
# Intake Process

Capture:
- business problem
- workflow impact
- expected value
- stakeholders
- governance requirements
- technical dependencies

---
# AI Use-Case Triage

| Category | Example |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Drafting and summarization |
| Knowledge Retrieval | RAG systems |
| Decision Support | Strategic analysis |
| Automation | Workflow orchestration |
| Autonomous Systems | Multi-step agents |

---
# Prioritization Criteria

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| Business Value | What measurable outcome exists? |
| Feasibility | Can this realistically be delivered? |
| Governance Risk | What operational exposure exists? |
| Scalability | Can patterns be reused elsewhere? |
| Strategic Alignment | Does this support enterprise priorities? |

---
# Capability Mapping

Map AI initiatives against:
- business capabilities
- operational workflows
- data dependencies
- integration requirements

---
# Technical Debt Management

Common debt categories:
- prompt sprawl
- duplicate tooling
- fragmented orchestration
- unmanaged retrieval systems
- undocumented workflows

---
# Governance Requirements

Portfolio management should include:
- evaluation standards
- funding visibility
- AI inventory management
- operational ownership
- lifecycle governance

---
# Common Failure Modes

- pilot graveyards
- no value measurement
- fragmented ownership
- excessive experimentation
- no portfolio visibility

---
# Strategic Guidance

Treat AI initiatives as:
- operational investments
- governance responsibilities
- portfolio assets

not disconnected experiments.
