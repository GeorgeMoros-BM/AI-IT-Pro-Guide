Define how AI capabilities are governed, funded, delivered, and operationalized across the enterprise.

---
# Core Principle

AI requires:
- ownership
- governance
- operational accountability
- lifecycle management

AI is not simply another software deployment.

---
# Operating Model Archetypes

| Model | Description | Strengths | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Centralized | Single AI team owns delivery | Strong governance | Bottlenecks |
| Federated | Shared governance, distributed execution | Scalability | Coordination complexity |
| Embedded | AI resources inside business units | Workflow alignment | Fragmentation |
| Hybrid | Central governance + federated delivery | Balance | Requires maturity |

---
# Recommended Enterprise Pattern

Most enterprises converge toward:
- centralized governance
- federated business execution
- shared AI platform services
- distributed operational ownership

---
# AI Center of Excellence (CoE)

## Typical Responsibilities

- governance
- platform standards
- evaluation frameworks
- architecture guidance
- enablement
- reusable accelerators

---
# Business Ownership

Business units should own:
- workflow outcomes
- operational adoption
- business value realization

IT should not own business transformation outcomes alone.

---
# IT Ownership

IT responsibilities:
- infrastructure
- governance
- integrations
- security
- platform operations
- observability

---
# Product vs Platform Model

## Product Model
Focus:
- user outcomes
- workflow effectiveness
- lifecycle management

## Platform Model
Focus:
- shared services
- infrastructure
- governance
- enablement

Most enterprises require both.

---
# Funding Models

| Model | Description |
|---|---|
| Central Budget | Shared AI investment |
| Business Unit Funding | Department-owned initiatives |
| Hybrid Chargeback | Shared platform + usage allocation |

---
# Governance Patterns

Required:
- AI steering committee
- governance council
- evaluation standards
- approved tooling portfolio
- risk classification

---
# Common Failure Modes

- unclear ownership
- fragmented AI tooling
- governance bottlenecks
- no operational accountability
- excessive pilot fragmentation

---
# Strategic Guidance

The objective is institutional AI capability, not isolated AI projects.
