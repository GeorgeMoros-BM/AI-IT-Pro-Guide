## Definition

AI governance is the operational system used to:
- control AI risk
- manage lifecycle accountability
- define operational standards
- maintain visibility
- enable scalable AI adoption

AI governance is not merely policy documentation.

It is:
- operational
- architectural
- organizational
- procedural

---
# Core Objective

Enable organizations to deploy AI safely and at scale without:
- operational chaos
- unmanaged automation
- uncontrolled vendor sprawl
- data leakage
- governance fragmentation

---
# Core Governance Domains

| Domain | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Strategy | Align AI with business priorities |
| Risk | Classify and manage exposure |
| Security | Protect data and workflows |
| Architecture | Standardize technical patterns |
| Operations | Govern lifecycle management |
| Legal | Address regulatory and contractual exposure |
| Workforce | Define enablement and acceptable use |

---
# Governance Principles

## Governance Should Scale With Risk
Low-risk workflows require lightweight controls.

High-risk workflows require:
- stronger review
- evaluation
- monitoring
- human oversight

## Governance Must Enable Adoption
Overly restrictive governance creates:
- shadow AI
- unauthorized tooling
- fragmented workflows

## Governance Requires Operational Visibility
Organizations cannot govern systems they cannot inventory or observe.
